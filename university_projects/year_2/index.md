## 🔹 Applied Design — Formula E Space Frame Chassis

A full engineering design project to develop a Formula E space frame chassis, following the complete design process from specification through to a physical prototype.

The project covered the full product development cycle including a Product Design Specification (PDS), Gantt chart, concept evaluation, simple truss analysis, and design communication. The final design was modelled in **SolidWorks** and physically constructed from balsa wood, this was then impact force tested to ensure the chassis met design requirements.

### Key Skills
- SolidWorks CAD modelling
- Engineering design process (PDS, concept evaluation, design communication)
- Structural analysis (simple truss analysis)
- Physical prototyping

## 🔹 Materials Science — Composite Materials in Formula 1

A two-part materials science study investigating composite materials for Formula 1 applications, combining theoretical analysis, experimental testing, and FEA simulation.

**Main Report** — Proposed an M40J/Epoxy composite for an F1 front wing, covering composite theory, material selection, cost-effectiveness, and sustainability. Theoretical Voigt Modulus calculations (rule of mixtures and 10% rule) were compared against experimental 3-point bend test data across three laminate orientations: [0]₁₆, [90]₁₆, and [±45]₄ₛ. The proposed composite achieved a theoretical modulus of **227.52 GPa**.

**FEA Study** — A supplementary finite element analysis simulating the FIA front wing deflection test (Article 3.15.4) on a 2026 F1 front wing modelled in SolidWorks 2020. Using M40J/Epoxy material properties, the simulation showed a maximum deflection of **3.2mm** — well within the 10mm regulatory limit.

### Key Skills
- Composite material theory (rule of mixtures, 10% rule, laminate theory)
- Material selection and justification
- Experimental testing (3-point bend test) and data analysis
- FEA simulation (SolidWorks Simulation 2020)
